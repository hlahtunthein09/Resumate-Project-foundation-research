01 — Starting point

People know their careers. They should not need to know CV writing.

The original problem came from making separate CVs for real job applications: repeated Canva edits, blank CV forms from copy shops when design tools were not accessible, and hours spent remaking a CV for each new position.

Personal experience

One career history, repeatedly rebuilt

During 2022–2023, the founder repeatedly made CVs in Canva for different applications. Each role required another manual version; without Canva knowledge, the practical alternative was a basic blank form with little guidance or presentation quality.

Observed user problem

People need help for every new role

A younger job seeker needed help applying to restaurant, office, receptionist, and optical-shop roles. Each application needed a different CV, creating repeated work for both the applicant and the person helping them.

Whom

Job seekers with unequal access

The intended users include people with limited English, limited technical knowledge, little CV knowledge, or no ability to pay someone else to make every CV for them.

Where

Inside the application process

ResuMate is used where CV creation, upgrading, tailoring, and variation become a barrier to applying for work.

How

No design-tool learning curve

A user can complete a guided form, talk with AI, or upload an existing CV instead of first learning Canva, CV structure, or AI prompting.

The user should provide career facts. ResuMate should turn those facts into professional, relevant, readable CVs—and preserve the work for reuse.

Research basis: founder’s Why / Whom / What / Where / How answers; Research Foundation §§1–2, 4, 9.

02 — Research approach

The research started with concrete user journeys, then tested those needs against product reality and external evidence.

01 / DISCOVERYFounder’s real CV-making and helper experiences
02 / USER FLOWSForm, AI chat, and existing-CV journeys
03 / REALITYExisting ResuMate prototype audit
04 / MARKETHands-on competitor and workflow study
05 / EVIDENCECV quality, ATS parsing, UX, and localization references

The discovery work established the actual needs: simple form entry, AI conversation in manageable chunks, editable suggestions for summaries and work experience, preview-before-generation, template selection, and preservation of uploaded originals. The later research cross-checked those needs against the prototype, competitors, professional guidance, ATS parsing constraints, and regional CV conventions.

This sequence matters: external products and references informed the research, but they did not replace the founder’s product intent or turn unresolved decisions into requirements.

Research basis: founder’s discovery answers and user journeys; Research Foundation §§0, 3, 28, 34–35.

03 — Evidence reviewed

Each research source answered a different question about the CV problem.

Area What the source established How it shaped the foundation
Product discovery Real repeated CV work, lack of Canva/English/CV knowledge, and the need to support both self-service users and helpers. ResuMate must be accessible without a design-tool learning curve and must preserve work for different job directions.
Existing product Existing upload, AI interview, job-description generation, versioning, and rendering flows, plus technical risks. Research could distinguish product intent from the actual prototype and identify what needs formal future design.
Competitors HelloCV’s broader AI-CV workflow; Teal’s reusable-content and tailoring pattern; Rezi’s diagnostic framing; Canva’s template variety. Reuse, tailoring, diagnostics, and visual choice are useful patterns—but ResuMate retains Version → Variation rather than copying another product model.
External practice Harvard’s guidance on specific, readable, well-organized CVs; Workday’s parsing constraints around sections, jobs, and dates. CV quality must balance professional writing, human readability, relevance, and machine parseability.
UX & context Chunked question/review patterns and the fact that regional CV expectations differ, including photo and personal-information conventions. Inputs cannot be one fixed sequence, and localization cannot be treated as an optional Western template setting.

Research basis: Research Foundation §28 and Appendix A.

04 — Competitor findings

Competitors supplied useful patterns. ResuMate remains the subject and retains its own model.

HelloCV

Useful as a feature and UX reference

Hands-on analysis covered AI profile creation, upload, editing, agent tools, job-related features, and more. It reinforces that existing CV import and AI-assisted workflows are important entry points.

Research distinction: observed flows do not replace ResuMate’s Version → Variation career-state branching.

Teal

Reusable foundation + job-context tailoring

Teal supports the insight that content can be selected, reordered, and reused rather than rewritten from zero. Tailoring should be driven by relevance to a specific job context.

Research distinction: its “master resume” pattern is evidence, not a reason to erase Version and Variation.

Rezi

Diagnostics, not an ATS promise

Rezi’s score explanation supports treating a score as a way to identify weaknesses and opportunities—not as the exact score an employer’s ATS will produce.

Canva

Visual expectations and template variety

Canva illustrates demand for different visual styles and template customization. It is a presentation reference, not a content model to copy.

Research basis: Research Foundation §§13, 15, 17, 21, 34; HelloCV feature-analysis repository.

05 — CV quality & ATS

A strong CV is both readable to a person and dependable for a system to parse.

Quality is multidimensional: there is no meaningful single number that can replace professional judgment, context, and evidence.

TruthfulnessProfessional writingSpecificityRelevanceReadabilityInformation hierarchyATS compatibilityVisual quality

Human readability

A reviewer should quickly understand the person’s identity, relevant experience, important skills, and evidence.

+

Machine readability

Clear sections, jobs, dates, and less risky formatting support dependable extraction.

ATS should be presented as explainable diagnostic guidance: parseability, structure, consistency, relevance, formatting risk, and improvement suggestions—not a magic universal score.

Research basis: Harvard Career Services; Workday Resume REST API; Rezi Score explanation; Research Foundation §§10–15.

06 — AI assistance & truth

AI can remove the writing barrier without inventing a career.

Some users can describe what they did and what they know but cannot produce polished English CV writing. That is exactly where ResuMate should help—while keeping every important claim grounded in user-provided evidence.

AI may transform supported facts

  • Write professional summaries and experience bullets
  • Organize, clarify, prioritize, and tailor content
  • Improve wording and section emphasis
  • Ask factual follow-up questions
  • Explain why a change was suggested

AI must not fabricate unsupported facts

  • Employers, responsibilities, or management experience
  • Metrics, revenue gains, team sizes, or achievements
  • Certifications, technologies, qualifications, or skills
  • Any claim lacking user evidence

Research basis: Research Foundation §9; original product discovery context.

07 — Input & existing CVs

CV creation is flexible. Job context is optional and may arrive in any order.

General CV

Career facts → professional CV

No target role, JD, or requirements are required to create or improve a base CV.

Targeted CV

Facts + role / JD → tailored CV

A target role, actual job post, requirements, or a JD pasted first can all provide useful context.

Existing CV

PDF, DOCX, image, or text can be used as a primary entry path.

Version

A meaningful base career state

A new Version is appropriate when professional identity changes substantially—such as education, skills, projects, experience, and direction moving from a 3D creative career to software development.

Variation

A derived CV for a particular context

A Variation comes from one Version and adapts it to a target role, actual hiring post, requirements, company context, or another tailoring goal.

Position ≠ Variation. One Frontend Developer role can produce different Variations for Company A, Company B, and Company C because each job description defines a different context.

The user chooses an Active CV Form—a Version or Variation currently used for job seeking. This is not a public profile. A Variation does not overwrite its parent Version.

Research basis: Research Foundation §§1, 6.1–6.5, 17.4, 19.

09 — Presentation & localization

Content state, template choice, and regional convention are separate concerns.

Content layer

Version / Variation changes the CV’s state and context.

It determines which real career facts, wording, selection, ordering, and job-specific emphasis form the CV.

Template layer

Template changes how content is presented.

It can change layout, typography, spacing, color, and photo/no-photo treatment without inherently changing the content source.

Localization is a first-class finding: photo, age, national/identity or passport-related information can be expected, unsuitable, or sensitive depending on region, industry, and employer/job context. Harvard’s U.S.-oriented guidance is valuable evidence, not a universal worldwide CV rule.

Research basis: Research Foundation §§13, 16, 19; Canva and Harvard references.

10 — Current scope boundary

The current foundation is CV-focused. Related career-platform features are later scope.

Strong MVP priority

  • Private User Profile
  • CV creation by form or AI/chat
  • Existing CV import and use as-is
  • Professional AI wording
  • Quality / ATS diagnostics
  • Role and JD-aware tailoring
  • Version and Variation management
  • Original preservation
  • Template switching and localization
  • PDF output and user review

Future / later phases

  • Job search engine and external job APIs
  • Application tracking and lifecycle
  • Live job-seeking agent
  • Public Portfolio website
  • Advanced public career profile concepts
  • Agency / enterprise multi-client workspace
  • Bulk agency workflows
  • External integrations and monetization refinement
